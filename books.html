Every book I've read

A running list of everything I've read, with a note or two on each one. Updated as I finish new books. No ratings — just honest impressions.

I started keeping this list because I noticed I was forgetting books almost as fast as I was reading them. Writing a line or two about each one forces me to actually think about what I took away, rather than just moving on to the next one. This isn't a recommendation list — it's a record.

2026

07

Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion

Robert B. Cialdini

The six principles — reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity — are so embedded in modern marketing that reading this feels like seeing the code behind the screen. Essential reading, even if you think you already know it.

Marketing
06

The Courage to Be Disliked

Ichiro Kishimi & Fumitake Koga

Adlerian psychology presented as a Socratic dialogue. Provocative in the best way — the chapter on "tasks of life" reframed how I think about responsibility. Some of it I pushed back on, which probably means it was doing its job.

Psychology

2025

05

Building a StoryBrand

Donald Miller

The central idea — your customer is the hero, not your brand — sounds obvious once you hear it. The seven-part framework is genuinely useful. I think about it every time I see a brand talking about itself instead of its customer.

Marketing
04

Thinking, Fast and Slow

Daniel Kahneman

Long. Dense. Worth it. The System 1/System 2 framework is one of those mental models that quietly changes how you see decision-making everywhere — in advertising, in conversations, in yourself. I'll be returning to this one.

Psychology
03

This Is Marketing

Seth Godin

The shortest path to understanding why mass marketing is over. Godin's best writing is when he's being provocative — "the smallest viable audience" is a concept that felt counterintuitive and then suddenly obvious. Good for early-career perspective.

Marketing
02

Atomic Habits

James Clear

Probably the most widely read self-improvement book of the last decade for a reason. The 1% improvement compounding idea is genuinely useful. What I appreciated most is that Clear doesn't moralize — he just shows the mechanics.

Self-improvement
01

Contagious: Why Things Catch On

Jonah Berger

Where I started. The STEPPS framework (Social Currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical Value, Stories) is a clean lens for analyzing what makes content shareable. Reading this during my internship gave me a vocabulary I didn't have before.
